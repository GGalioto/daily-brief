2. 🎓 Business School — Module 30: Objection Handling

"Objection handling — turning 'that sounds expensive' / 'we already do this' into information, not a wall to push through." Second module in Arc 9 (Closing the First Real Deal), opened yesterday with discovery.

Gong's analysis of over 300 million cold and discovery calls found something counterintuitive: nearly half of all objections are dismissive brush-offs that have more to do with timing and relevance than the actual product. Most reps hear an objection and treat it as a verdict. It's usually a request for more information wearing a verdict's clothes.

The standard framework for handling this, going back to Jack Carew's 1990 book and still the industry default in 2026, is called LAER: Listen (all the way through, no interrupting), Acknowledge (the concern, without conceding or arguing), Explore (ask an open question to find the real root cause), Respond (with something specific to what they actually said, not a canned rebuttal). The step almost everyone skips — and the one that actually closes deals — is Explore. Sales teams trained on LAER with real example scripts saw close rates improve 15–30% depending on the study, one specific case citing a 26% jump in a single quarter after training.

On the classic "that sounds expensive": the research is consistent that this is very rarely actually about the number. Nine times out of ten, "expensive" means the value story hasn't landed yet — the person doesn't yet see enough return to justify what's being asked, and dropping the price doesn't fix a story problem. The fix is the Explore step: "help me understand — expensive compared to what you're doing now, or expensive for what it'd need to solve?" The answer to that question is the real objection. "We already do this" gets the same treatment: it's not actually a wall, it's a request to explain what's different, and Explore surfaces whether "already doing this" means "with a person" or "well" or "cheaply" — three completely different real objections hiding under one sentence.

Worked example: not a live conversation yet, but the cheat sheet gets written now

Two LAER responses, written now instead of improvised under pressure:

  • "We already have Cari for this": Listen fully, Acknowledge ("Cari clearly knows this process cold"), Explore ("what happens to that knowledge on the day she's out sick, or eventually moves on?" — this is Module 26's delegation-vs-abdication question, not a sales line), Respond only after hearing the answer — don't pre-load a rebuttal.
  • "That sounds expensive": Listen, Acknowledge ("that's a fair thing to ask before anything's even been quoted"), Explore ("expensive relative to what — what does it currently cost when a deadline gets close, in time or in risk?"), Respond with real numbers only if they exist — and today, honestly, they don't yet, which is fine to say directly.
