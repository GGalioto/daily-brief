2. 🎓 Business School — Module 29: Running Discovery

"Running discovery — the questions that surface the real problem (and the real budget) before you ever describe a build." This opens a new arc (Arc 9: Closing the First Real Deal), added today now that Arc 8 wrapped 8/6.

A Gong study of 519,000 real Business-to-Business (B2B) discovery calls found top-performing salespeople ask 11–14 questions in a 25–35 minute call — go past 14 and it stops feeling like a conversation and starts feeling like an interrogation, and conversion drops. The framework behind good questions is called SPIN: Situation (where things stand now), Problem (what's actually broken), Implication (what that costs when it goes wrong), Need-Payoff (what solving it would be worth). The single biggest finding from that study: on deals above $50,000 Annual Contract Value (ACV), Implication questions were the strongest predictor of whether the deal closed — and top performers asked four times as many of them as average reps.

This is Module 15's "don't describe what you'd build" rule, made mechanical. Naming a build teleports a stage-1 buyer straight into evaluation before trust exists — Module 15 called that out back in July. Discovery is the actual practice of not doing that: you ask, they answer, and the room stays in their world instead of yours.

Worked example: next week's Rainbow visit

Terri already reframed the visit correctly, unprompted: "let Cari prove AI can't do what she does." That's a live Situation/Problem environment, not a pitch meeting. Four questions, one per SPIN stage, built directly off that frame:

  • Situation: "Walk me through what happens from a job coming in to a notification getting filed — who touches it, in what order?"
  • Problem: "What happens when Cari's out — sick day, vacation? Has a deadline ever actually gotten close?"
  • Implication (the highest-value question, per Gong): "If a filing genuinely got missed, what's the real list of consequences — is it a fine, a stop-work order, both?"
  • Need-Payoff: "If someone else had a live copy of what's currently only in Cari's head, what would that actually be worth to you?"

The expired-certification finding from Section 1 fits naturally as a follow-up Problem question — "has cert renewal ever come up as an issue?" — rather than an announcement. Let them supply the words; that's the whole method.
