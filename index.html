2. 🎓 Business School — Module 23: Basic Bookkeeping & Taxes for a Sole Proprietor/LLC

The problem this module solves: most solo founders treat bookkeeping as a February problem. It isn't — it's a daily-habit problem that becomes an expensive scramble precisely because nobody built the habit in month one.

The one rule that prevents 80% of the pain

Separate business money from personal money, completely, from day one. One dedicated business checking account, one business card, zero personal purchases on either. This single habit is cited across multiple 2026 small-business accounting guides as the #1 bookkeeping mistake and the single fix that prevents it — because once personal and business spending blend in one account, every deduction becomes defensible only with reconstructed memory instead of a clean record, and if the Ohio LLC's liability shield is ever tested, commingled funds are exactly the evidence a court looks for to say the LLC wasn't really being run as a separate entity ("piercing the corporate veil").

What actually has to be tracked, starting now

  • Every dollar in. Not just what clears the business account — cash, Venmo, whatever. The IRS taxes income based on what you earned, not what you remembered to log.
  • Every dollar out, with a receipt or record. A missing receipt doesn't just risk an audit — it can disqualify the deduction outright even if the expense was completely legitimate, because the IRS's standard is documentation, not honesty.
  • Mileage, if it applies. A logged trip to Rainbow's office for the Thursday lunch, if it becomes a recurring pattern, is a real deductible business mile — but only if it's actually logged (date, purpose, miles), not reconstructed from memory at tax time.

The one that surprises first-year founders: quarterly estimated taxes

A W-2 job withholds tax automatically, every paycheck. A sole proprietor or single-member LLC (which is how Corvino Forge LTD is very likely taxed by default, unless an S-corp election was separately filed) has no employer withholding anything — the business owes the IRS directly, four times a year, not once. If you expect to owe $1,000 or more in federal tax for the year, quarterly estimated payments are required, and underpaying triggers a penalty even if the full balance gets paid correctly at filing time — the penalty is for the late timing of the payment, not the final total being wrong.

Worked example — Corvino Forge's actual shape right now

This one's honest, not hypothetical: as of today, Corvino Forge LTD's business income is close to $0 (Mark's HVAC retainer isn't yet fully live-billing, CashMoney hasn't sold, Rainbow hasn't signed anything). That actually matters for this module — right now there's no quarterly-payment obligation because there's no profit yet to owe tax on. But the habit-building has to start before the income does, not after, because:

  • Every dollar already spent this year — GHL's $104.57/mo, Namecheap hosting, any tool subscriptions — is a real, deductible business expense if it's tracked now, and effectively lost as a deduction later if it isn't (reconstructing 8 months of subscription charges from memory in March is exactly the scramble this module exists to prevent).
