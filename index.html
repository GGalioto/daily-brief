2🎓 Business School — Module 28

Documentation as a business asset, not paperwork — why "it's all in my head" is a hidden liability.

Most people think documentation is overhead — the thing you write after the real work is done, mostly to cover yourself. That's backwards. Undocumented knowledge is a liability sitting on the balance sheet whether or not anyone's written it down: it caps how fast you can grow (nobody else can do the work), it caps what the business is worth if you ever sell it (Module 24 — a buyer prices the risk of you disappearing), and it caps how much you can delegate without also handing over judgment you can't take back (Module 26).

The test that separates real documentation from busywork: could someone competent but unfamiliar pick this up and do it correctly without asking you a question? If the answer's no, what you have isn't documentation — it's a note to yourself.

Worked example, live and walking into a room with you today: Cari's entire filing operation at Rainbow is the textbook version of undocumented process — personal eBiz PIN, a dry-erase board, deadlines tracked in one person's head. That's not a staffing problem, it's a documentation problem wearing a staffing costume. The moment she drops to 3 days a week, the business's actual operating knowledge drops with her, because none of it exists anywhere else. Module 24 already named this as a real acquisition-value discount; today it's the reason "cheaper than Cari 3 days a week" (Brian's own words) is a real budget signal and not a throwaway line.

The mirror version, inside your own build: your own multi-agent pipeline just proved the same lesson from the other side. Yesterday's validation-layer work only closed clean because three independent passes checked it against the actual written spec — not against each other's say-so. And this week's reliability research on multi-agent AI systems (Section 6 below) makes the same point at industry scale: most multi-agent failures trace back to spec ambiguity, not infrastructure — teams fail because nobody wrote down, precisely, what "done" means. Documentation isn't the boring part of the build. It's the part that keeps the build from silently rotting the moment nobody's watching it directly.

Applied to today: if the delegation conversation with Terri goes anywhere near "how would this actually work," the honest one-line answer is the same one Module 26 landed on — you're not selling automation, you're selling a second copy of the judgment that currently only lives in Cari's head.
